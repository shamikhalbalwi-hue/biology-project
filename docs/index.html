What is a healthy diet?

The World Health Organization (WHO) says a healthy diet follows four ideas. Healthy diets look different in every country. These four ideas stay the same.

Adequacy

You get enough of the nutrients your body needs. Then you do not become deficient.

Balance

You do not eat too much or too little of any one thing.

Moderation

You limit foods with a lot of unhealthy fat, sugar, and salt.

Diversity

You eat many different foods. Different foods give different nutrients.

Six easy swaps

Tap a card to see the better choice and the reason.

Sugary drinks are one of the main sources of added sugar for teenagers, says the US CDC. WHO advises that free sugars stay below 10% of your daily energy.

Try this: Add a slice of lemon or a few mint leaves to cold water.

Whole grains still have their fiber, vitamins, and minerals. WHO lists whole grains as part of a healthy diet.

Try this: Choose brown rice, oats, or whole-wheat bread.

WHO advises less than 5 grams of salt per day. That is a little less than one teaspoon. Too much salt can raise blood pressure. Nuts also give healthy unsaturated fats.

Try this: Keep a small bag of unsalted nuts or an apple in your school bag.

WHO says unhealthy fats should be limited. Frying adds a lot of fat. Baking, grilling, and steaming add less.

Try this: Ask for grilled chicken or baked potatoes instead of fried.

Fruit has sugar, but it also has fiber and vitamins. WHO says to keep free sugars low. Free sugars are the sugars that are added to food and drinks.

Try this: Eat dates, grapes, or banana when you want something sweet.

The US CDC says eating breakfast is linked to better memory, better mood, and fewer missed school days.

Try this: Eat an egg, a bowl of oats, or whole-wheat bread with fruit.

Do not forget protein and variety

Your muscles need protein to grow. Good choices are beans, lentils, fish, eggs, and nuts. WHO also says to replace saturated fats with unsaturated fats. You find these in fish, avocado, nuts, and most plant oils.

Eat many colors of fruits and vegetables. Each color gives different nutrients.
